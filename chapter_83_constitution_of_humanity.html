Solar System Jurisprudence · Volume VIII · Chapter 83

The Constitution of Humanity: A Proposed Constitution for the Solar System

Abstract. A proposed constitutional framework for humanity’s future expansion throughout the Solar System, founded on stewardship, dignity, constitutional symmetry, peaceful exploration, intergenerational justice, scientific freedom, and accountability.

Preamble

We, the people of Earth, acting in trust for ourselves, our descendants, and all future members of the human family, establish this Constitution to preserve liberty, justice, peace, scientific inquiry, responsible stewardship, and the common inheritance of the Solar System.

We affirm that law exists not merely to regulate power but to preserve civilisation across generations.

Article I — The Constitutional Grundnorm

The Solar System shall be held in stewardship for present and future generations of humanity.

No generation possesses absolute ownership of natural resources whose significance extends beyond its own lifetime.

Every institution exercising authority under this Constitution acts as trustee rather than proprietor.

Article II — Constitutional Principles

This Constitution shall be interpreted consistently with human dignity, equal justice, peaceful exploration, scientific freedom, accountability, intergenerational stewardship, environmental responsibility, transparency, subsidiarity, and constitutional symmetry.

Article III — Rights of Humanity

Every person possesses equality before the law, freedom of conscience and speech, freedom of scientific inquiry, due process, peaceful participation in government, access to justice, and protection from arbitrary authority.

Article IV — Duties of Humanity

Every person owes duties to future generations, the environments of Earth and other celestial bodies, peaceful cooperation, preservation of knowledge, and responsible use of common resources.

Article V — Stewardship of the Solar System

The planets, moons, asteroids, and other naturally occurring celestial resources constitute the common inheritance of humanity.

Exploration and responsible commercial activity may occur subject to this Constitution.

Article VI — The Stewardship Reserve

A permanent constitutional reserve shall support science, constitutional institutions, education, planetary defence, peaceful exploration, and preservation of civilisation.

Article VII — Institutions

The institutions shall include a Solar System Assembly, a Council of Member Governments, a Solar System Constitutional Court, an Auditor-General, an Independent Scientific Commission, and a Stewardship Trust.

Article VIII — Separation of Powers

Legislative, executive, and judicial authority shall remain institutionally independent. Checks and balances shall be preserved.

Article IX — Scientific Responsibility

Scientific inquiry shall remain free. Governments shall neither suppress legitimate scientific investigation nor compel scientific conclusions.

Article X — Constitutional Finance

Public resources shall be administered transparently, prudently, sustainably, and for long-term public benefit.

Article XI — Peace

The Solar System shall be explored primarily for peaceful purposes. Planetary defence shall be recognised as a legitimate collective responsibility.

Article XII — Constitutional Review

Every institution remains subject to judicial review. The Constitution shall remain the supreme law.

Article XIII — Amendment

Amendment shall require broad democratic participation and consideration of future generations. Human dignity, stewardship, accountability, judicial independence, and peaceful exploration may not be abolished.

Article XIV — Interpretation

This Constitution shall be interpreted according to its text, structure, purpose, stewardship, constitutional symmetry, and preservation of civilisation.

Closing Declaration

Humanity’s greatest inheritance is the capacity of free people to govern themselves wisely.

The measure of this Constitution will be the trust it preserves between generations, the justice it secures among peoples, and the stewardship it exercises over the Solar System.

Author: Roderick J. Young, B.Sc., B.Soc.Sc.(Hons), P.G. Dip. (Public Policy), LLB.

This chapter is an original jurisprudential proposal and does not state existing law.
