Solar System Jurisprudence · Volume VIII · Chapter 84

The Institutions of the Solar System Commonwealth

Abstract. This chapter proposes a constitutional architecture for a future Solar System Commonwealth founded on stewardship, democratic accountability, judicial independence, scientific freedom, and intergenerational responsibility.

1. Introduction

No constitution exists independently of the institutions that administer it. Principles without institutions become aspirations; institutions without principles become instruments of power.

The objective is not to replace existing nation-states but to govern matters that concern humanity as a whole.

2. Constitutional Authority

The Commonwealth derives authority from the Grundnorm that humanity holds the Solar System in trust for present and future generations.

Every institution remains subject to the Constitution, judicial review, public accountability, and periodic democratic renewal.

3. The Solar System Assembly

The Assembly shall enact Commonwealth legislation, approve the budget, supervise institutions, establish scientific priorities, oversee stewardship of common resources, and ratify major amendments.

4. The Council of Member Governments

The Council shall coordinate member governments, emergency response, treaty implementation, strategic planning, and conflict resolution.

5. The Executive Commission

An independent Executive Commission shall implement legislation, administer programmes, coordinate scientific missions, manage the Stewardship Reserve, and maintain public administration.

6. The Solar System Constitutional Court

The Court shall interpret the Constitution, review legislation, determine institutional disputes, and issue advisory opinions.

7. The Stewardship Trust

The Trust shall preserve strategic reserves, invest for future generations, support science and education, and publish annual audited reports.

8. The Scientific Commission

An Independent Scientific Commission shall evaluate major proposals, advise institutions, publish reports, promote cooperation, and preserve research integrity.

9. The Auditor-General

The Auditor-General shall examine public expenditure, stewardship assets, procurement, institutional performance, and financial risk.

10. The Electoral Commission

The Electoral Commission shall supervise elections, campaign finance, electoral boundaries, voting technology standards, and independent audits.

11. The Office of Future Generations

A Commissioner for Future Generations shall review legislation, assess long-term consequences, and possess standing before the Constitutional Court.

12. Emergency Powers

Emergency powers must be declared, necessary, reviewable, supervised, and temporary. Permanent emergency government is incompatible with constitutional stewardship.

13. Transparency

Meetings should be public, budgets published, decisions reasoned, and records archived except where confidentiality is clearly justified.

14. Constitutional Culture

A constitutional culture requires civic education, scientific literacy, public participation, ethical leadership, respect for evidence, and peaceful disagreement.

15. Conclusion

The Commonwealth provides machinery through which humanity may govern matters that no single nation can govern alone.

Its strength will be measured not by territory but by the trust it preserves for every generation that follows.

Author: Roderick J. Young, B.Sc., B.Soc.Sc.(Hons), P.G. Dip. (Public Policy), LLB.

This chapter is an original jurisprudential proposal and does not state existing law.
