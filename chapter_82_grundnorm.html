Solar System Jurisprudence · Volume VIII · Chapter 82

The Grundnorm of the Solar System: From Legal Positivism to Constitutional Symmetry

Abstract. Every legal system rests upon an assumption that cannot itself be proven by that legal system. This chapter argues that humanity stands at the threshold of a new constitutional era in which stewardship and constitutional symmetry become the foundation of Solar System Jurisprudence.

1. Introduction

Every generation inherits a legal system. Few generations are required to invent one.

The twenty-first century presents such a moment. The Industrial Revolution required commercial law. The Atomic Age required international law. The Information Age required cyber law. The Space Age requires Solar System Jurisprudence.

The question is not simply who owns the Moon, Mars, or the asteroid belt. The deeper question is whether existing jurisprudence possesses the constitutional foundations necessary to govern a civilisation that no longer lives upon one planet.

2. The Foundation of Positive Law

Legal positivism asks a deceptively simple question: Why is law binding?

Thomas Hobbes answered that law derives from the sovereign. John Austin argued that law is the command of the sovereign backed by sanctions. Hans Kelsen proposed the Grundnorm. H L A Hart replaced sovereign command with the Rule of Recognition.

Despite their differences, these theories share an Earth-centred political frame. Solar System Jurisprudence proposes that this limit is historical rather than permanent.

3. The Constitutional Problem

Every constitution answers three questions: Who governs? What may they govern? For whose benefit do they govern?

Does Parliament own the Moon? Can one nation own an asteroid? Can one generation consume resources formed billions of years before humanity existed?

These questions require a deeper constitutional principle than an extension of nineteenth-century property law.

4. Constitutional Symmetry

A legal principle should retain its essential character regardless of the scale at which it is applied unless a compelling reason requires otherwise.

Justice should remain coherent across the individual, family, community, nation, humanity, and the Solar System.

5. The Solar Grundnorm

Humanity holds the Solar System in trust for present and future generations. No generation possesses absolute ownership of natural resources whose significance extends beyond its own lifetime.

Ownership becomes stewardship. Power becomes trusteeship. Authority becomes responsibility.

6. Resonance and Legitimacy

Used as a jurisprudential metaphor, resonance describes a legal system understood by citizens, fairly administered by institutions, consistently applied by courts, and sustainably inherited by future generations.

Political conflict, constitutional uncertainty, excessive litigation, and declining public confidence are symptoms of jurisprudential dissonance.

7. Time as Constitutional Responsibility

Every statute creates obligations beyond the lives of its legislators. Every mine exhausted today cannot be mined tomorrow. Every constitutional error becomes an inheritance.

Justice is not merely spatial. Justice is temporal.

8. The Stewardship Reserve

A modest proportion of strategic Solar System resources may be constitutionally reserved for civilisation itself.

Illustratively, one percent of recoverable gold, silver, and thorium may support science, education, peaceful exploration, planetary defence, and preservation of humanity’s common inheritance.

9. Professor Mannheim’s Challenge

Professor Philip Mannheim’s work suggests that unexplained observations may require reconsidering governing equations rather than adding invisible entities.

Applied to law, fragmentation may indicate constitutional incompleteness rather than administrative failure. The search begins not with more law but with deeper law.

10. Predictions

If Constitutional Symmetry is sound, it should produce greater long-term policy stability, improved stewardship, reduced conflict over extraterrestrial resources, and stronger intergenerational equity.

11. Conclusion

For humanity’s future, the largest constitutional question may become stewardship of an entire Solar System.

The purpose of law is to preserve ordered liberty across space, generations, and civilisation itself.
